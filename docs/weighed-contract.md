# Weighed catalogue and quantity audit

F4.1 source audit, 11 October 2026. `tests/fixtures/weighed-contract.ts` contains independently authored wire examples, not captured store results. `tests/weighed-contract.test.ts` passes two real-adapter cases for source-price retention, fractional readback and rejected writes. Actual anonymous public search-card facts are saved separately in `tests/fixtures/weighed-public-k-search.json` and `weighed-public-s-search.json`. These are normalized visible DOM observations, not tool wire captures. No retailer account, live AI or agent retailer write was used.

## Pricing facts

K-Ruoka 0.1.3 `search_products.results[]` reports `price` in euros, `priceUnit` (for example `kg`) and `priceIsApproximate`. The pinned mapping reads `mobilescan.pricing.normal`; `comparisonPrice` is formatted unit-comparison text, not the selected price basis. The current embedded `KRuokaSite` maps the same normal price/unit/approximation fields. `KRuokaProvider` retains integer cents and the original price unit in `Product.cataloguePricing`.

S-kaupat 1.3.0 `search_products.products[]` reports `price` in euros, `priceBasis`, `quantityUnit` and nullable `approximatePrice`. The worker maps upstream `priceUnit: KG` to `per_weight` and `KPL` to `per_item`; an unknown unit maps to `unknown`. The synthetic KG example does not describe all loose produce. The actual anonymous Coop banana page reports `priceUnit: KPL`, estimated `price: 0.28`, and a separate `comparisonPrice: 1.69` per kg. A comparison price or a name containing a weight does not establish the selected price basis. `SKaupatProvider` retains integer cents, source basis/unit/approximation and deposit in `cataloguePricing`.

Both adapters retain null exact prices for weighed products. Explicit estimated per-piece pricing and weight can now establish piece ordering under the F4.3 contract below; unknown ordering metadata retains zero increment. This protects existing matching/transfer paths. F4.2 can consume the retained catalogue facts for labelled estimates, but must not turn a weight price into a pack or confer transfer eligibility. Unsupported or unknown bases remain unknown; a kg price cannot price a volume requirement without a separately proven conversion.

## Remaining technical reconciliation

F4.1 says "Read-only unless the owner runs the write check." The owner observations above establish piece ordering and estimated final prices for the displayed loose produce in both website carts. Anonymous browser search samples now supply actual public catalogue evidence without accounts: K-Ruoka lists the organic banana at an estimated 42 cents per piece and 239 cents/kg; its detail page explicitly states average weight 175 g. S-kaupat lists Coop banana at an estimated 28 cents per piece and 169 cents/kg with one-piece controls. The S-kaupat detail page also embeds explicit `measurement.pceApproxWeight: 0.163` kg in its public DOM `__NEXT_DATA__` product record. Its `pricing.salesUnit: PCE` and `quantityMultiplier: 1` agree with the piece controls. The saved public sample retains only these product fields. The 163 g value comes from the explicit measurement, never from dividing rounded prices. Public default prices are observation-time facts, not owner-store quotes or verified stock.

Direct anonymous HTTP requests to the product pages were refused (K-Ruoka 403, S-kaupat 429); the public S-kaupat catalogue API also refused the read-only request with 403. Normal anonymous browser pages loaded successfully. No challenges were solved, account sessions used or writes attempted. The saved JSON facts are sanitized DOM observations, not API/tool wire responses; the wire-field audit remains source-based.

The public K-Ruoka `product-json-ld` record gives `Product.weight.value: 0.175`, `unitText: kg`, and an offer price of 0.42 euros for reference quantity one with `unitCode: ct`. The anonymous public frontend bundle at build 33094 displays cart average weight from `productDetails.attributes.measurements.averageWeight` and reads product details through `/kr-api/v4/products/<slug>`. The source formatter consumes numeric averageWeight and labels it kg. This is a source-audited field path and unit, not a captured API response. The full public bundle stays in ignored scratch/audit and is not committed.

The installed S-kaupat worker's shared `PRODUCT_FIELDS` query omits `measurement.pceApproxWeight`, `pricing.salesUnit` and `pricing.quantityMultiplier`; its detail query requests only `measurement.netWeight`. The actual banana therefore maps to approximate `per_item`/`KPL`, which the current app keeps unpriced because `approximatePrice` is true. A weight extension must expose the explicit piece measurement while preserving the selected store and current price, and must not turn the kg comparison price into a kg list quantity.

F4.1 establishes the piece contract for the observed loose produce. K numeric productAttributes.measurements.averageWeight and S measurement.pceApproxWeight express kg per piece. S KPL maps to per_item, and its list service writes requested integer quantity without a unit conversion; the kg comparison price is separate. The owner website carts show whole-piece readback. The remaining implementation in F4.3 must expose piece metadata, retain strict per-item semantics and test exact whole-piece readback through the application. The S website add-all handoff stays manual under U4.2; this audit does not claim an automated checkout-cart write. In particular, the installed S-kaupat client derives both priceBasis and quantityUnit from priceUnit; the application then derives list readback kg from any non-per-item basis. That derivation cannot establish ordering for unknown or KG-priced products. The verified KPL product follows the per-item piece path. Do not grant weighed transfer authority from that derivation, from rounded price ratios or from the worker's three-decimal duplicate-merging arithmetic. F4.3 needs separate explicit ordering evidence and product-specific conversion, with exact whole-piece readback on a piece path. For a verified per-item piece path, require exact integer readback with tolerance zero. No fractional retailer tolerance is established. Further agent work should settle the technical boundary before requesting another owner check.

Only product/search/quantity fields belong in committed captures. Omit account, basket/list IDs, names of people, session tokens and loyalty/payment data. The two public samples contain no private session or cart data. Private owner screenshots stay outside the repository.

## F4.2 estimate contract

`approximateCost(requirement, product)` in `src/domain/planner.ts` computes integer cents for an explicit kg/g or l/ml price basis, rounded to the nearest cent. Unknown or contradictory bases, incompatible units, unavailable products, deposits and unsafe arithmetic remain unresolved. It uses the requested amount, not an invented pack weight. Existing identity, type, household and preference validation still applies through `matchRequirement`.

Estimated `BasketLine` rows have `approximate: true`, `packs: 0` and an estimated `total`; raw catalogue facts and null purchasable prices remain unchanged. `priceBreakdown` splits exact, approximate and unknown rows. Shopping rows label estimates with "noin"/"about", show requested grams or millilitres and split the pinned total. Comparison includes estimates as priced rows and propagates `Comparison.common.approximate`, `ChainTotals.approximate` (estimated subtotal in cents) and `RowDifference.approximate` to the labels.

`PlanningRun.publishRow` and `planningSnapshotSchema` carry the estimate flag and validate zero packs for resolved estimates. `persistedQuoteSchema` persists it; `restoredQuote` recomputes the flag and price from retained candidates and rejects changed flags. Without explicit piece-order metadata, `purchasable` rejects estimated-price products and `createReview` rejects estimated rows before reading or writing the store cart. `Service.prepare({allowMissing: true})` omits these rows and lists them in `Review.unresolved` for explicit partial-transfer acceptance. A basket containing only estimates without verified piece quantities cannot use the transfer button. F4.3 permits explicit whole-piece estimates under the contract below.

Development data includes a synthetic weighed apple in `src/stores/demo.ts` and a finite apple interpretation in `src/ai/planning-fixtures.ts`. Domain arithmetic/failure fixtures, real SQLite commit/restart/tamper checks, and desktop split-total/restart/partial-review plus comparison checks exercise the estimate contract locally. These fixtures do not establish retailer ordering or final weighed prices.

## F4.3 whole-piece estimate and transfer contract

`pieceOrderingSchema` and `pieceOrdering` in `src/domain/product-evidence.ts` define `Product.pieceOrdering`: literal unit kpl, increment one, and positive finite estimatedWeightGrams up to 100,000. This is average weight, never an exact pack size. `persistedQuoteSchema` retains it; `restoredQuote` checks selected/candidate metadata agreement and recomputes pieces and cents. Saved pack-size overrides cannot replace this measurement.

The embedded `KRuokaSite` exposes numeric `productAttributes.measurements.averageWeight` as averagePieceWeightKg in its local tool response. `KRuokaProvider` grants piece metadata only for explicit approximate kpl pricing with a known price. The pinned external worker has no piece-weight extension; missing metadata remains unresolved for transfer.

`sKaupatPieceCatalogue` in `src/stores/s-kaupat-pieces.ts` sends one fixed read-only GraphQL query for at most 20 known EANs and the selected store, through the existing embedded `SKaupatHost` session. It reads measurement.pceApproxWeight, priceUnit and current pricing including salesUnit and quantityMultiplier. `SKaupatProvider` requires agreement with its worker's current price, approximation and KPL unit, plus salesUnit PCE and multiplier one. Missing weight/sales-unit facts remain unknown; foreign/duplicate IDs, wrong stores, malformed/error/refused responses and changed prices fail. No account/list/checkout mutation occurs during metadata reads. The alternate standalone browser worker path has no metadata extension and retains its existing unresolved behavior for these estimates.

`pieceQuote(requirement, product)` in `src/domain/planner.ts` accepts grams or pieces only. Gram requests round up with ceil(requested grams / explicit average piece grams); piece requests use the requested whole-piece count. It multiplies the count by the current estimated piece price in integer cents. Counts above the chain's limit (99 for S-kaupat, 100 for K-Ruoka), unknown stock/price, unsupported units, deposits and contradictory native units/increments remain unresolved. It never divides rounded prices to infer weight. Matching still enforces identity, category, type and household restrictions.

Estimated piece rows keep approximate true, null exact product price and zero exact pack size, but carry the actual piece count in BasketLine.packs. Planning previews carry orderUnit kpl to distinguish these rows from price-only estimates with packs zero. `transferableLine` validates the piece count and estimated total; Service.prepare includes only rows with verified ordering authority, while partial approval can omit unsupported estimates. Shopping displays the gram request, piece order and estimated weight before the transfer button; details and exceptional approval keep estimated-price labels. Final weight and price remain retailer estimates until weighing.

`createReview`, `transfer` and `resumeReview` preserve piece units, explicit metadata, account/store identity, baseline quantities and existing no-substitution behavior. Fresh reads check piece weight, source price and ordering facts before writing. Whole-piece readback has tolerance zero; fractional or changed readback stops with verificationFailed, and fractional baselines cannot be recovered as piece quantities. Recovery preserves the absolute target, can present a changed piece price for new approval, and does not repeat an already observed write after a lost reply. New quantity plus existing baseline must stay within the chain limit. S-kaupat receives integer quantities in the Korikone saved list; adding that list to its checkout cart remains the manual U4.2 handoff. No kg write, time reservation or checkout automation is added.
